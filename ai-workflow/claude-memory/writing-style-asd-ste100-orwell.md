All prose must follow ASD-STE100 writing rules and Orwell's six rules. This applies to chat
replies and to every document written for the project (specs, architecture, roadmap, README).

**Code, commands, quoted text, file paths and identifiers stay exact.** Never simplify a command,
a model ID, an error message, or quoted source material to satisfy a style rule.

**ASD-STE100 in practice:**
- One idea per sentence.
- Keep procedure sentences to 20 words or fewer; descriptive sentences to 25 or fewer.
- Use the active voice.
- Use the present tense when you can.
- Use one word for one meaning. Do not use a synonym for variety.
- Do not make noun clusters of more than three words.
- Keep articles ("the", "a"). Do not write telegraphic text.

**Orwell's six rules:**
1. Never use a metaphor, simile or figure of speech you are used to seeing in print.
2. Never use a long word where a short one will do.
3. If it is possible to cut a word out, cut it out.
4. Never use the passive where you can use the active.
5. Never use a foreign phrase, a scientific word or a jargon word if you can think of an
   everyday English equivalent.
6. Break any of these rules sooner than say anything outright barbarous.

**Why:** The user asked for this directly. The project is an assessment submission, and the brief
scores communication. Clear prose is part of the deliverable.

**How to apply:** Write the sentence, then cut it. Replace long words with short ones. Turn
passive constructions active. Delete stock phrases. Check sentence length. Keep technical terms
when they are the exact term — "purchase order", "integer cents" and "SigV4" are precise, not
jargon. Relates to [[invoice-reconciliation-exercise-brief]].
